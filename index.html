Color Realism

🌙

Micro-detailing and density. In color realism, the artist has to place pigment in a way that conveys the texture of skin, the shine of eyes, or the silk of petals without damaging the epidermis. At the same time, the ink has to be packed densely; otherwise, the tattoo may heal with gaps. Depth control. Skin has a different thickness on every part of the body. If color pigment is placed too deeply, or the same area is worked too many times, it can blur or cause healing issues. If it is placed too superficially, the pigment may fall out after healing. Anatomical placement. The human body is not a flat sheet. The design has to fit perfectly into the curves of the muscles so that when the body moves, the image does not distort and remains alive and harmonious. Color realism is always a union of an impeccable artistic foundation, premium pigments, and the mathematical precision of the artist.

Korean Style

✨

Korean style and color micro-realism are delicate, gentle tattoos with soft shades and ultra-fine lines that look like airy watercolors on the skin. This is an extremely complex, jewelry-level technique requiring exceptional skill, flawless precision, and working at the absolute limit of possibilities.
