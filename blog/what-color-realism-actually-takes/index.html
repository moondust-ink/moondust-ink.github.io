What color realism actually takes

Color realism looks effortless in a finished photo, but almost none of the work is visible in the final result. Every gradient you see is built from dozens of thin passes of pigment, layered slowly enough that the skin can take it without scarring or blowing out the color.

The hardest part isn't drawing the reference — it's translating it onto a living, moving surface. Skin stretches, heals, and settles over weeks, so contrast and saturation have to be planned for how the piece will look in a month, not just on the day it's finished.

This is why a color realism consultation takes real time. We talk through placement, size, and how the design will move with your body before a single line goes down.
